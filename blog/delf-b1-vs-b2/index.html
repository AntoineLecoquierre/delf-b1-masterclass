Choosing your level

DELF B1 vs B2: which one should you take?

Antoine L. Native French Teacher, Paris 7 min read

Choosing between the DELF B1 and B2 is really one question: what do you actually need the diploma for? Take a level higher than your goal and you waste months; take one lower and it may not count. This guide explains the real difference between the two, how the exams compare, and how to pick with confidence.

B1 and B2 on the CEFR scale

Both are part of the CEFR (the Common European Framework), the standard scale from A1 (beginner) to C2 (mastery). The jump from B1 to B2 is the jump from coping to convincing:

  • B1 — independent user (threshold). You handle everyday situations, describe experiences, give simple opinions, and manage most things that come up while travelling or living in France.
  • B2 — upper-intermediate (vantage). You argue a case, defend a point of view with nuance, follow longer and more abstract discussions, and understand detailed articles and reports.

How the two exams compare

The structure is the same — four sections, each out of 25, pass at 50/100, with a 5/25 minimum in every section. What changes is the difficulty of the content and the depth expected.

DELF B1DELF B2
Listening & readingFamiliar, concrete topicsLonger, more abstract, argumentative
WritingOpinion essay, ~160–180 wordsArgued/formal text, ~250 words
SpeakingInterview, role-play, simple point of viewStructured argument, defend it under questioning
Feels like"I can get by and express myself""I can argue and persuade"
In one line: B1 asks can you communicate? — B2 asks can you argue and be convincing? If you can already defend an opinion against push-back, you're near B2. If that still feels hard, B1 is the right target.

Which one does your goal need?

Here are the most common reasons people take the DELF, and the level usually involved:

  • Travel, living abroad, personal achievement: B1 is a strong, motivating milestone that proves real independence.
  • Work using French day-to-day: B1 for many customer-facing or practical roles; B2 where you negotiate, write reports or lead meetings.
  • University in France (degree programmes): usually B2, sometimes higher depending on the course.
  • Residency, immigration & citizenship: B1 has long counted for several pathways; B2 is increasingly required for French citizenship. These rules change often.
Important: official language requirements for visas, residency and citizenship change regularly and differ by country and situation. Never rely on a blog for this — always confirm the current requirement with the official body handling your case (e.g. the relevant government or immigration authority) before you book an exam.

Our honest recommendation

If your goal only requires B1, take B1: it's faster, cheaper, and a genuine achievement. If you need B2 for university or citizenship, aim for B2 — but consider passing B1 first as a stepping stone. Clearing B1 builds exam technique and confidence, and almost everything you learn (the format, the scoring logic, the four-skill method) carries straight over to B2.

Frequently asked questions

Is DELF B1 or B2 harder?

B2 is clearly harder. B1 tests familiar, everyday situations; B2 requires arguing, defending an opinion and understanding longer, more abstract material.

Which level do I need for citizenship or university?

It depends on the goal and the year. B1 has counted for several residency pathways; B2 is common for university and, increasingly, citizenship. Always confirm the current official requirement for your case.

Should I take B1 first or go straight to B2?

If you only need B1, take B1. If you already handle opinions and longer texts comfortably, B2 may be within reach — and passing B1 first is a solid stepping stone.
