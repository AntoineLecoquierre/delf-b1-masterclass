DELF B1 Practice Test: how to use one properly

Antoine L. Native French Teacher, Paris 7 min read

Doing practice tests is the fastest way to raise your DELF B1 score — but only if you use them properly. Most learners "do" a paper, glance at the answers and move on, and wonder why nothing improves. A practice test is a diagnosis, not a quiz. Here's how to squeeze real progress out of every one.

Where to find real practice tests

Start with the source. France Éducation international — the official body behind the DELF — publishes free sample papers (sujets exemples), including the audio for the listening section. These are the closest thing to the real exam, so use them as your benchmark. Around them, a good prep course gives you extra full-length papers with answer keys so you don't run out after two attempts.

One rule: always practise with real exam-format material. Generic "French quizzes" won't prepare you for the DELF's specific question types (true/false with justification, the 160–180 word essay, the three-part oral).

Recreate exam conditions

A practice test only works if it feels like the real thing. Time pressure is part of the exam, so train it:

  • Time each section — roughly 25 min listening, 35 min reading, 45 min writing.
  • Play the listening audio only twice, exactly like exam day. No pausing, no rewinding.
  • Phone away, no dictionary. You won't have them in the exam.
  • Write your essay by hand and count the words at the end.

The part everyone skips: the correction

This is where the score actually improves. After each paper:

  1. Mark it honestly with the answer key. Each section is out of 25; you pass at 50/100 with a 5/25 minimum per section.
  2. For every mistake, write down why. Was it a vocabulary gap? A trap (a self-correction, a negation)? Running out of time? The pattern tells you what to fix.
  3. Redo the questions you got wrong a few days later. If you can't now get them right, the lesson hasn't landed yet.
Grade writing and speaking differently. There's no single right answer — compare your work against a model answer and the scoring grid, not a key. Ask: is it on topic, the right length, well organised, full of connectors?

What a practice question looks like

  • Listening: "À quelle heure ouvre le musée le dimanche ?" — a specific detail you catch on the second listen.
  • Reading: a true/false statement where you must copy the sentence from the text that proves your answer.
  • Writing: "À votre avis, faut-il limiter les voitures en ville ? (160–180 mots)."
  • Speaking: draw a short document on screen time, prepare for 10 minutes, then give your point of view.

A simple practice rhythm

Quality beats quantity. Two or three full papers, done properly and corrected carefully, are worth more than ten skimmed ones. In your final week, sit at least one complete timed mock — all four sections — then spend your remaining time on your weakest section only.

Frequently asked questions

Are there free DELF B1 practice tests?

Yes — France Éducation international publishes free official sample papers (with listening audio). They're the closest thing to the real exam.

How many practice tests should I do?

Two or three full papers done properly beat ten skimmed ones. Always do at least one complete timed mock before the exam.

How do I mark my own test?

Use the key for listening and reading (25 per section, pass at 50/100, 5/25 minimum each). For writing and speaking, compare against a model answer and the criteria.
