DELF B1 Speaking

DELF B1 Speaking Exam: tips & example answers

Antoine L. Native French Teacher, Paris 8 min read

The speaking test (production orale) is the part most learners dread — and the one where a little strategy pays off fastest. It's only about 15 minutes long, it's completely predictable, and with the right phrases prepared in advance you can walk in calm. Here's exactly how it works, part by part, with example answers you can adapt.

How the oral is structured

You meet one examiner for roughly 15 minutes, after 10 minutes of preparation for parts 2 and 3. The whole thing is scored out of 25. Three parts, back-to-back:

PartWhat it isPrepWith examiner
1. Guided interview
Entretien dirigé		A short conversation about youNone~2–3 min
2. Role-play
Exercice en interaction		You solve a real-life situation with the examinerIncluded in the 10 min~3–4 min
3. Point of view
Expression d'un point de vue		You react to a short document and give your opinion10 min~5–6 min

Part 1 — the guided interview

The examiner asks simple questions about you: your studies or job, your family, your hobbies, your plans. There's no preparation, but there's a secret: the questions barely change. Prepare a 40-second self-presentation that naturally uses three tenses, and you've already won this part.

Example self-presentation: « Je m'appelle… et je suis étudiant(e) / je travaille comme… En ce moment, j'apprends le français parce que… L'année dernière, j'ai commencé à étudier seul(e), et l'année prochaine, je voudrais passer le DELF pour… » — one sentence in the present, one in the past, one in the future. That single move shows range.

Useful phrases to buy time and sound natural:

  • C'est une bonne question… — that's a good question (gives you a second to think).
  • Ce que j'aime le plus, c'est… — what I like most is…
  • Si je me souviens bien… — if I remember correctly…

Part 2 — the role-play

You draw a situation and act it out with the examiner: booking something, returning a faulty product, convincing a friend, sorting out a problem. Your job is to interact — ask, react, negotiate, stay polite.

Phrases that carry almost any role-play:

  • Ask politely: Est-ce que vous pourriez… ? / Je voudrais…
  • Propose: Et si on… ? / Je vous propose de…
  • Object politely: Je comprends, mais… / Le problème, c'est que…
  • Persuade: Ce serait vraiment mieux si… / Ça vaut la peine parce que…
  • Close: D'accord, c'est parfait, merci beaucoup !
Golden rule: don't just answer — drive the exchange. Ask the examiner a question back. A candidate who keeps the dialogue alive scores far higher than one who waits to be prompted.

Part 3 — expressing a point of view

You draw a short document (a few lines on a familiar topic — screens, food, transport, work) and get 10 minutes to prepare a short talk. Then you present your opinion for a few minutes and answer the examiner's follow-up questions.

Use the same reliable structure as the writing essay, out loud:

  1. Introduce the theme and your position: Ce document parle de… À mon avis…
  2. Argument 1 + example: Tout d'abord… Par exemple…
  3. Argument 2 / the other side: Cependant… / D'un autre côté…
  4. Conclude: Pour conclure, je pense que…
Use your 10 minutes well: don't write full sentences — you won't have time to read them. Jot down 4–5 keywords per idea and one example. You want to speak, not recite.

Five habits that raise your score

  • Keep talking. Silence costs more than a small mistake. If you're stuck, rephrase.
  • Self-correct out loud. « …il a allé — pardon, il est allé. » That shows control, not weakness.
  • Vary your connectors. d'abord, ensuite, par contre, donc, enfin — they make you sound organised.
  • Add one example per idea. Concrete examples are what push a B1 answer above the pass line.
  • Record yourself. The single fastest fix. Hearing your own voice removes half the exam-day nerves.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the DELF B1 speaking exam?

About 15 minutes with the examiner, plus 10 minutes of preparation for parts 2 and 3.

What if I make a mistake?

A few mistakes are expected at B1. Fluency matters more than perfection — and correcting yourself out loud actually shows control.

Can I prepare my self-presentation in advance?

Yes. Part 1 always asks about you, so prepare a 40-second self-presentation using past, present and future, and rehearse it until it feels natural.
